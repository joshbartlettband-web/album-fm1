# ALBUM 01

Album is a series of firmwares for the M-VAVE FM-1, one per record. Each edition is a few deep sounds and the
photographs they came from, and each sound moves its photo as you play. Edition 01 follows one record: chords bent while they ring (glide guitar), heard mostly through a reverse reverb.

## 1. Install

Try it first in the browser emulator: the same code, with sound. To install, open the install page in Chrome or Edge
with the FM-1 connected by USB. The installer saves a backup first and can put M-VAVE's firmware back.

## 2. Playing

The keys play. PRESET (the big knob) picks the patch, and with it the photo. The screen is the photo; a patch's name
shows at the top for a moment when you choose it, and a knob's value shows at the bottom while you turn it.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Keys | Play: a note each, or a chord each in chord mode. Velocity counts. |
| PRESET | The next or previous patch (and its photo) |
| K1 to K4 | Glide, Swell, Tone, Blur |
| ARP | Chord mode off or on: it is on from the start (lit while on). Held, then a key: that key's note becomes the key (section 5) |
| SELECT | Chord mode's key, a semitone a click |
| ALGORITHM | Fuzz, the fifth knob (section 3) |
| FX | The fuzz in or out (lit while in), its setting kept |
| SEQ | The patch's beat on or off (blinks with it). Held: ALGORITHM sets the tempo, SELECT the beat's level |
| PLAY | Hold: the notes keep sounding after you let go (lit green). Again: let go. |
| OCT- / OCT+ | The octave, two down to two up |
| HOME | This patch's five knobs and tempo back where it starts |
| SAVE | Keep everything (lit when something changed): section 8 |
| REC | Link to another FM-1 (section 7). Held a second: Bluetooth off or on |
| MASTER | Volume |

![Red Door with a chord held: the photo wavers sideways with the pitch](img/screen-playing.png "Playing: the photo moves with the sound")

## 3. The five knobs

Every patch has the same five knobs: K1 to K4, and ALGORITHM for the fuzz. Each moves the sound and the photo together, and each patch keeps its own settings.

| Knob | The sound | The photo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K1 Glide | How far the pitch wanders: the whole chord bent down and let back up, as a guitar's tremolo arm does, and each string drifting on its own | The rows waver sideways while a note sounds |
| K2 Swell | The reverse reverb's length: a quick lift to a slow rise over a second and a half | The red light swells |
| K3 Tone | Dark to bright, the reverb too | Darker to brighter |
| K4 Blur | How much of the sound is the reverb, and how smeared: clear to all wash | A sideways smear |
| ALGORITHM Fuzz | A fuzz pedal on the whole chord at once, so its notes grind together, before the tremolo and the reverb. Clean to all fuzz; the lows stay clean beside it, and Tone opens it up. FX takes it out and puts it back | The colours burn: more contrast, more colour |

![Blur turned up: its value at the bottom](img/screen-knob.png "A knob's value while it turns")

The warm light in each photo (a fire, the sun, a lamp) flickers with the sound's level.

## 5. Chord mode

Chord mode is on from the start: each key plays a whole chord. ARP turns it off for single notes, and on again. The chords come from My Bloody Valentine's records. Kevin Shields has
said the progressions are "quite basic"; what makes them sound like his is the open tunings, which "leave a lot of
room for odd variations of a basic chord", with open strings that "clash against the others in interesting ways"
(Guitar World, March 1992). So the chords here are simple ones made strange: suspended, with added ninths and
sixths, fifths without thirds, slash chords over a held bass note.

Every key plays something, the black keys too. In the key of C:

| Key | Chord | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C | C | I, home |
| C# | Db | the flat second, a darker turn |
| D | Dm | ii |
| D# | Eb | the flat third |
| E | Em | iii |
| F | F | IV |
| F# | D/F# | the major second, over its third |
| G | G (Gsus4 on some patches) | V |
| G# | Ab | the flat sixth |
| A | Am | vi |
| A# | Bb | the flat seventh |
| B | G/B | V over its third, in place of a diminished chord |

The flat seventh, flat sixth, flat third and major second are all on Only Tomorrow. The chord's name shows on the
screen as you play it.

**The key.** Turn SELECT to move the key a semitone a click, or hold ARP and press any key: its note becomes the
key, and chord mode stays on. The key shows at the bottom right. Chords you are holding move with it.

**Each patch voices the chords its own way,** so the same keys play differently on every patch:

| Patch | Its chords |
| --- | --- |
| RED DOOR | An open tuning's shapes: root, fifth, ninth and tenth, with the key's home note ringing over every chord like an open string; V suspended |
| MAPLE | An organ's close chords over a low root: sixths on the major chords, sevenths on the minor |
| DAHLIA | Fuzz chords: root, fifth, octave, the third high above |
| FLAME | Low and sparse: root, fifth and a tenth, nothing to blur the flicker |
| CLEMATIS | Spread wide: added ninths with the sixth on top; V suspended |
| WHIRL | Clusters: the second packed against the third |
| WINDOW | An electric piano's sevenths and ninths; the borrowed chords as dominant sevenths |
| PASTURE | Root, octave and tenth, each voice adding its own fifth; V suspended |
| BLUESTEM | An arpeggio: the chord's notes one at a time, up and down, in time with the beat |
| SNOWFALL | Sixths and ninths, close at the top |
| TOWER | Open chords, each voice doubled an octave up |

**Holding two chords** sounds both. A note they share sounds once and keeps ringing until you let go of both, as an
open string rings through a change of chord.

**Voices.** There are twelve (eight on one core: section 10). A chord takes three to six. When a new note needs a
voice and none is free, it takes the quietest voice that is fading out, whose sound is mostly in the reverb by then;
then a voice that only HOLD is keeping; only then the oldest note still under a finger. A key never stays silent.
If the FM-1 ever has to drop a voice or two to keep up, they come back a couple of seconds after the load eases.

## 6. The beat

Press SEQ and the patch's beat starts; press it again to stop. Each beat is soft and muffled, under the wash rather
than over it, and some of it goes into the reverse reverb, so its hits swell in backwards. The kick lifts the photo's
light a little.

| Patch | Tempo | The beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RED DOOR | 86 | Half-time and heavy, a snare roll into every fourth bar (Only Shallow opens on one) |
| MAPLE | 70 | A pulse so muffled it is barely there, brushed (after Is This and Yes) |
| DAHLIA | 100 | A quietly punchy backbeat (after Only Tomorrow) |
| FLAME | 100 | One figure over and over on low toms (after Nothing Is) |
| CLEMATIS | 64 | A brush and a breath, buried deep in the wash (after To Here Knows When) |
| WHIRL | 98 | A loose, swung dance groove (after Soon) |
| WINDOW | 80 | A rainy night's slow, swung beat on a rim |
| PASTURE | 72 | A heartbeat and a brush |
| BLUESTEM | 168 | A double-time break, quiet and far off (after wonder 2) |
| SNOWFALL | 64 | Almost nothing gets through the snow |
| TOWER | 104 | Scattered late-90s dance drums, kept soft (after In Another Way) |

Hold SEQ and turn ALGORITHM to change the tempo; each patch keeps its own, and HOME puts it back. Hold SEQ and turn
SELECT to set the beat's level, apart from the rest of the sound: five steps a click, a bar at the bottom like the
knobs'. It starts at 50; 100 is twice as loud, 0 silent. (Turning a knob while SEQ is held leaves the beat running when you let go.) Changing patch keeps the beat's place: the new patch's beat and tempo take over on the next
sixteenth. In chord mode on BLUESTEM, the arpeggio follows the same clock, with or without the beat.

## 7. Two FM-1s as one

Two FM-1s running Album link over Bluetooth and play as one: the keys, the knobs, the patch and hold go from each to
the other. In chord mode the chord's notes go across, so the other FM-1 plays the same chord. Start the beat on one
and the other starts its own beat in time with it, following its clock, and stops when it stops. Each unit drifts in pitch on its own, so together they detune against each other like two guitars.

1. On one FM-1, press REC: LINK LOOKING.
2. Turn ALGORITHM to choose the other FM-1 when its name shows.
3. Press REC again: LINKING, then LINK CONNECTED. LINK stays at the top right while the link is up.

A computer or a tablet can connect over Bluetooth MIDI too, at the same time. Album links to a stock FM-1 as well
(it plays its own sounds); that has not been tried on hardware yet.

Bluetooth adds a little delay. The slow-swelling patches hide it well.

## 8. Saving

SAVE keeps the patch you are on, every patch's knobs and tempo, the octave, chord mode and its key, whether the fuzz is in, and the beat's
level. They come back at the next power-on. Saving
stops the sound for a moment (the flash is written), so save between phrases.

## 9. MIDI

In over USB, Bluetooth and the TRS jack; out over USB and Bluetooth. Any channel.

| Message | In | Out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Note on and off | Plays | The keys (in chord mode, each chord's notes) |
| CC 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 | Glide, Swell, Tone, Blur, Fuzz | K1 to K4, ALGORITHM |
| CC 64 | Hold | PLAY |
| Program change | The patch (0 is RED DOOR) | PRESET |
| CC 120, 123 | All sound off | |
| CC 21 | The beat's level | SEQ held and SELECT |
| Clock, start, stop | The beat follows another FM-1 or a computer | While this FM-1's beat runs |

Nothing received is sent back out, so two linked FM-1s never echo.

## 10. The second core

The FM-1's chip has two cores. Album runs half of the voices and the reverse reverb on the second one. If the second
core does not start, stalls, or gets its results wrong (some FM-1s' second cores now and then misread memory), Album
gives it up and plays everything on one core until the next power-off, with up to eight voices instead of twelve.
You may hear one short gap when that happens.

Hold OCT- while switching on to start on one core.

## 11. Troubleshooting

- **No sound:** turn MASTER up. Check that Blur is not all the way up on a patch with a long Swell and a note just
  played: the reverse reverb takes its time.
- **The FM-1 restarted after a crash:** it starts on one core until the next power-off. Tell us what you were
  doing.
- **Back to M-VAVE's firmware:** the install page has a button for it.
